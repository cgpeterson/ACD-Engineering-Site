Mint Backup

Privacy Policy

Effective October 7, 2026

The short version: Mint Backup copies one folder from your computer into storage you own. Your backup is encrypted on your computer before it leaves. We do not run servers that receive your files, we cannot read your backups, and we never sell or share your data.

What Mint Backup is

Mint Backup is a Windows application that automatically backs up a folder you choose to a location you choose: Google Drive, Microsoft OneDrive, Dropbox, or a local or external drive. Everything runs on your computer.

Data the app handles

  • Your files. The contents of the folder you choose are read on your computer, compressed, encrypted (AES-256) and written to your chosen backup location. The encryption key stays on your computer and with the person who set up the app for you; it is never sent to us.
  • Sign-in tokens. When you connect a cloud account, the access token from that provider is stored on your computer, protected by Windows' built-in encryption. It is used only to read and write your backup.
  • Search index, previews and logs. To let you search your backups and recognise versions at a glance, Mint Backup keeps an index of file names and text, and small preview pictures of your files, on your computer. Diagnostic logs stay on your computer too.
  • Backup status signal (optional). If the person who set up Mint Backup turned on alerts, the app sends a short "backup succeeded" or "backup failed" signal to a monitoring service they chose, so they can be told if backups stop. This signal contains no file names or file contents; like any internet request, it reveals your computer's IP address to that service. When Mint Backup is set up, and when you set it up again on a new computer, the same service also receives a locked copy of your backup key. Only the person who set up Mint Backup can unlock it, so that they can help you get your files back if your computer is lost.

Google user data

If you connect Google Drive, Mint Backup requests only the drive.file permission, which allows it to create files and access only the files it has created. It cannot see, read, change or delete any other file in your Google Drive.

  • What is accessed: the encrypted backup files Mint Backup creates in your Drive, and your Drive's storage usage so the app can warn you if space runs low.
  • How it is used: solely to store, verify, and restore your backups, at your direction.
  • Sharing: Google user data is not shared with, transferred to, or sold to anyone. It is not used for advertising, and it is not used to train AI or machine-learning models.
  • Storage: backup files live in your own Google Drive. Sign-in tokens live only on your computer.

Mint Backup's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What we do not do

  • We do not collect analytics, telemetry or usage tracking.
  • We do not show ads.
  • We do not sell, rent or share personal information.
  • We do not operate any server that receives your files.

How long data is kept

Mint Backup keeps versions of your files for 90 days of use, then removes them from your backup automatically. Days your computer is switched off don't count, so time away never uses up your history, and nothing is removed sooner than 90 days. Your backup stays in your storage until you delete it.

Your choices

  • Remove Google Drive access at any time from your Google Account's third-party connections.
  • Delete your backup by deleting the MintBackup folder from your storage.
  • Uninstall Mint Backup from Windows Settings → Apps; this removes the app and the data it keeps on your computer, and gives back its Google Drive access. Your backup stays in your storage.

Children

Mint Backup is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them.

Changes to this policy
